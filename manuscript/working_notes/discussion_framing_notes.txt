================================================================================
DISCUSSION FRAMING NOTES: Survey vs. Experimental Paper Comparison
================================================================================
Based on analysis of: "Habitat Quantity Drives Community Assembly and Feedbacks
to Coral Condition in Reef Systems" (Stier, Primo, Curtis, Osenberg)
Prepared: March 2026

These notes guide how to frame the survey manuscript's results in relation to
the companion experimental study.

================================================================================
1. SCALING CONTRAST: Redirection (survey) vs. Field of Dreams (experiment)
================================================================================

Key framing: The two results are COMPLEMENTARY, not contradictory.

- Experiment: Added new habitat patches (1, 3, 6 corals) to bare reef for 17
  months. CAFI colonized proportionally with habitat quantity (FoD) at the
  community level, but with substantial species-level heterogeneity.

- Survey: Measured established communities on mature colonies spanning a wide
  range of sizes. Found sublinear scaling (Redirection, beta = 0.52) — larger
  corals have more CAFI but at declining per-unit density.

- Reconciliation: The experimental paper itself (lines 376-395) argues that
  post-settlement density-dependent processes can modify initial colonization
  patterns. Competition for space within the coral, territory defense by
  resident species, and resource limitation can shift communities from FoD
  toward Redirection over time. The survey captures the long-term equilibrium
  endpoint of exactly this process.

- Useful framing: "Initial colonization follows Field of Dreams, but
  established communities converge on Propagule Redirection as density-
  dependent interactions sculpt the community over time."

- Species-level: Both studies show heterogeneity. The experiment found some
  species FoD, some Redirection, some super-linear. The survey found 7/10
  Redirection, 3/10 FoD, 0/10 super-linear — consistent with the expectation
  that post-settlement processes push most species toward Redirection.

================================================================================
2. NULL CONDITION RESULT: Why the survey finds no CAFI → condition effect
================================================================================

The experiment found PC1_CAFI → PC1_Coral (significant, p = 0.032) and
condition declined at higher coral density (p = 0.017). The survey finds
NO CAFI metric predicts condition. Discuss these potential explanations:

a) OBSERVATIONAL CONFOUND (most important to discuss)
   - In the experiment, coral number was randomized → causal inference possible
   - In the survey, coral size is naturally varying → healthier corals may grow
     larger AND attract more CAFI, creating a confound
   - Controlling for volume (as we do) is necessary but may not be sufficient
     if volume, CAFI, and condition share unobserved confounders

b) TEMPORAL SNAPSHOT vs. ACCUMULATION
   - Experiment tracked cumulative effects over 17 months
   - Survey captures a single time point — may miss dynamic feedbacks
   - If CAFI effects on condition operate through sustained exposure to
     beneficial/harmful associates, a snapshot may not detect them

c) SIZE vs. NUMBER: DIFFERENT ECOLOGICAL AXES
   - Experiment varied the number of habitat patches (landscape-level density)
   - Survey varies individual patch size (colony-level)
   - CAFI effects on condition may operate through inter-colony density
     (competition among CAFI populations from different corals) rather than
     within-colony community composition
   - The survey's n_neighbors null result (Q4) complicates this — but the
     neighborhood survey only covered 61/114 corals with limited density range

d) POSITION CORRECTION (acknowledge as conservative)
   - The survey corrects physiology traits for stump_length + nubbin_length
     to remove the tissue gradient sampling artifact
   - This is the right approach, but if tissue quality genuinely covaries with
     branch position for ecological reasons (e.g., light exposure, flow), the
     correction may remove some real biological signal
   - This makes the null result conservative (harder to detect a real effect)

e) RAREFIED RICHNESS CONTROL (survey's key methodological advance)
   - The survey demonstrates that the apparent richness → condition effect
     (p = 0.018) is an abundance artifact: rarefied richness shows no
     relationship (p = 0.45)
   - This is a contribution the experiment did not test

3. SPECIES-LEVEL COMPARISON
================================================================================

Shared species between studies (and their effects):

Experiment (Table S2):
  Positive: Caracanthus (p_adj=0.017), Harpiliopsis (NS), Periclimenes (NS),
            Dascyllus flavicaudus (NS), Dascyllus aruanus (positive, associated
            with enhanced condition on solitary corals), Calcinus latens (NS)
  Negative: Luniella pugil (raw p=0.014), Alpheus diadema (raw p=0.042),
            Chlorodiella barbata (associated with condition decline)

Survey (with expanded key species): 10 key species tested (Trapezia
  punctimanus, T. rufopunctata, Stegastes nigricans, Alpheus lottini,
  Synalpheus charon, Harpiliopsis depressa, Periclimenes spp., Calcinus
  spp., Abudefduf spp., Galeropsis monodonta). Effect directions mostly
  match experiment but none survive Hochberg FWER correction. Trapezia
  and fish effects positive (as expected); Galeropsis negative.
  - Direction matches and divergences to report
  - Only Caracanthus was significant in experiment after correction
  - Survey should report: even with 4 additional species from experiment,
    no species shows significant condition effect in established communities

Framing: "The experimental finding that Caracanthus abundance positively
predicts condition was not replicated in established communities, suggesting
the effect may operate through initial colonization dynamics rather than
sustained presence."

================================================================================
4. HOLOBIONT CONCEPT (from experimental paper lines 443-458)
================================================================================

Both papers support expanding the coral holobiont concept to include
macrofaunal associates. Key language from the experiment:

"Expanding the holobiont concept to include macrofaunal associates — as dynamic
contributors whose roles depend on context — provides a more comprehensive
framework for understanding coral demography and designing effective
restoration interventions."

The survey adds: the context-dependence extends to the spatial scale at which
CAFI effects operate. Colony-level CAFI composition may not predict condition
(survey), but landscape-level CAFI dynamics can (experiment). This suggests
macrofaunal effects on coral health are emergent properties of the seascape,
not reducible to within-colony community composition.

================================================================================
5. RESTORATION IMPLICATIONS (where both papers converge)
================================================================================

- Experiment: Solitary corals had BETTER condition than clustered corals,
  driven by CAFI composition shifts (more beneficial associates on isolated
  colonies)
- Survey: No CAFI metric predicts condition in established communities,
  but coral size (not neighborhood density) is the dominant predictor of
  CAFI abundance and richness

Together: Restoration designs should consider both colony spacing (to avoid
CAFI-mediated condition decline from the experiment) and colony size (to
support diverse CAFI communities from the survey). The survey's sublinear
scaling means that several medium-sized colonies support more total CAFI than
one equivalent-sized large colony — relevant for outplanting strategy.

7. METHODOLOGICAL ALIGNMENT WITH COMPANION EXPERIMENTAL PAPER (March 2026)
================================================================================

The following changes were made to align the survey's analytical methods with the
companion experimental study (Stier, Primo, Curtis, Osenberg), ensuring methodological
consistency for companion papers:

a) GROWTH NOT MEASURED IN SURVEY (legitimate difference)
   - The experiment includes growth_vol_b (unified allometric growth) as a 5th
     variable in the condition PCA (protein, carbs, zoox, AFDW, growth)
   - The survey is a single-timepoint observational study with no growth data
   - Survey condition PCA uses 4 variables (protein, carbs, zoox, AFDW), all
     position-corrected for sampling artifact
   - This is a legitimate difference: the survey captures physiological status,
     not growth dynamics. Discuss in Methods and acknowledge in Discussion.

b) SQRT TRANSFORM ON COUNT PREDICTORS
   - Experiment uses sqrt(abundance) in species-level condition models
   - Survey now applies sqrt() to all count-based CAFI predictors (total_cafi,
     n_trapezia, n_resident_fish, n_galeropsis, key species counts)
   - Continuous indices (PC1_CAFI, Shannon, richness) are NOT sqrt-transformed
   - Rationale: down-weights high-abundance outliers, consistent with experiment

c) HOCHBERG (FWER) CORRECTION FOR KEY SPECIES TESTS
   - Experiment uses Hochberg stepwise correction for species-level tests
   - Survey now uses Hochberg (FWER) for key species condition tests
   - Other test families (CAFI→condition, condition→CAFI) retain BH (FDR)
   - Since the survey's null results are robust, this change has no practical
     effect on conclusions but ensures methodological consistency

d) COMMUNITY TRANSFORMATION SENSITIVITY (Part A3)
   - Experiment tests 3 community matrix transformations: Sqrt, Sqrt+CS, Hellinger
   - Survey now tests all 3 transformations for PC1_CAFI → condition
   - Additionally tests a filtered PCA (species with >=10 presences AND >=10
     total individuals), mirroring experimental species filtering
   - Result: null finding is robust across ALL transformations and filtering

e) BALANCED SITE SUBSAMPLING FOR PERMANOVA (Part 4D)
   - Experiment runs 500 balanced subsampling iterations to test PERMANOVA
     robustness to unequal treatment sample sizes
   - Survey now runs 500 balanced subsampling iterations across 5 distance metrics
   - Equal n drawn from each site (n = min site count = 35) per iteration
   - Result: site effect on composition is robust to sample-size imbalance
