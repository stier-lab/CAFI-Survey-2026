### Issues Found

1. **[ABS-01]** severity: major
   - Problem: The opening sentence leads with mechanism rather than ecological problem. "The per-capita density of organisms in biogenic habitats determines the intensity of occupant--host interactions -- nutrient subsidies, predator defence, tissue consumption -- that shape habitat condition." This is a claim about density, not an entry point for a broad audience. A JAE reader from community ecology needs a hook into why the habitat-size → occupant scaling question matters before they care about per-capita density mechanics.
   - Location: Sentence 1
   - Proposed fix: "The abundance of organisms in biogenic habitats -- corals, kelps, trees -- scales predictably with habitat size, yet whether that scaling is proportional or diluted across larger patches carries consequences for the habitats themselves." (Then sentence 2 can introduce per-capita density as the mechanism.) Alternatively, open with the general pattern and frame the tension immediately: "Larger biogenic habitats support more occupants, but whether those occupants accumulate proportionally or are diluted across growing patches shapes the intensity of interactions that feed back on the habitat itself."
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only

2. **[ABS-02]** severity: major
   - Problem: Gap sentence ("yet the chain from habitat size through community assembly to habitat condition has rarely been quantified") is too vague and passive. It does not name the specific missing link (the composition step), does not explain *why* it matters, and buries the tension inside a compound sentence. The Introduction's gap is sharper: three links untested (sublinear scaling in established communities, turnover vs. nesting, community variation → condition).
   - Location: Sentence 2 (second clause)
   - Proposed fix: Promote the gap to its own sentence and make it concrete: "Yet whether the functional form of that scaling — proportional versus diluted — shapes community composition, and whether the resulting community variation feeds back on habitat condition, remains unresolved." Or more concisely: "Whether sublinear scaling generates compositional turnover, and whether that turnover feeds back on habitat condition, has not been tested."
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only

3. **[ABS-03]** severity: major
   - Problem: Q2 result has no quantitative number. "Community composition turned over rather than nested along the size gradient" reports only a direction, not a magnitude. The db-RDA result (7.8% explained after partialing site, F₁,₁₀₈ = 9.74, p = 0.001) and the NODF non-significance (p = 0.28) are concrete and available.
   - Location: Sentence 5
   - Proposed fix: "Colony volume explained 7.8% of community composition after accounting for reef site (db-RDA, p = 0.001), with turnover rather than nestedness along the size gradient (NODF p = 0.28)." This is 24 words and can replace or expand the current sentence.
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only

4. **[ABS-04]** severity: minor
   - Problem: "Propagule Redirection" is jargon introduced without a one-phrase gloss. A JAE reader unfamiliar with the coral-fish colonization literature may not know what it means. The abstract later mentions "post-settlement processes drive the transition to sublinear scaling," which provides context but comes after the term is used.
   - Location: Sentence 4
   - Proposed fix: Add a parenthetical gloss on first use: "consistent with Propagule Redirection (density dilution as habitat competes for a finite colonist pool)." This adds ~9 words but eliminates the jargon barrier.
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only

5. **[ABS-05]** severity: minor
   - Problem: The So-What beat (final sentence) hedges twice and splits into two separate implications (restoration spatial planning + climate vulnerability). The double hedge ("potential implications," "may be disproportionately") weakens the close. The broader cross-system implication — which the Discussion uses to anchor the paper's generality — is absent.
   - Location: Final sentence
   - Proposed fix: Pick one implication and state it more directly, then add the cross-system closing move that appears in the Discussion. Example: "These results trace a feedback from habitat size through community assembly to habitat condition — a chain relevant wherever biogenic habitats are restructured by disturbance or restoration — and suggest that density dilution may compound the vulnerability of large corals during thermal stress events." Removes one hedge, adds the cross-system claim, stays under 250 words if ABS-03's fix is applied.
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only

6. **[ABS-06]** severity: minor
   - Problem: Sentence 7 (companion experiment) consumes ~25 words to report a contrast finding that is discussed at length in the manuscript but is peripheral to the abstract's main story. It partially breaks the 5-beat arc by inserting a supplementary result between the Q3 result and the So-What.
   - Location: Sentence 7 ("These patterns contrast with a companion colonization experiment...")
   - Proposed fix: Compress to one clause appended to the Q1 result: "...consistent with Propagule Redirection, a contrast to proportional scaling observed during initial experimental colonization that implicates post-settlement processes." This frees ~15 words for the composition number (ABS-03).
   - Ripple risk: none — abstract only
