### Issues Found

1. **[DISC-01]** severity: **major**
   - Problem: Two new quantitative claims appear in the Discussion that are absent from Results. The opening of the Colony Size/Heatwaves section states "a doubling of volume increases CAFI abundance by only ~43% (2^0.52^ ≈ 1.43)" and "mutualist services…at diminishing per-volume intensity as colonies grow (2^−0.48^ ≈ 72% per doubling of volume)." These are new derived quantities never reported in Results and present as evidentiary claims.
   - Location: Discussion section "Colony size, heatwaves, and the feedback loop," paragraph 1 ("The three questions we address form a hypothesized feedback…")
   - Proposed fix: Either (a) add a single sentence to the Q1 Results reporting the per-doubling figures explicitly, or (b) reframe the Discussion sentence as an interpretive gloss on the reported exponent: "The sublinear exponent (β = 0.52) means a doubling of colony volume increases CAFI abundance by only ~43% — a direct geometric consequence of the exponent already reported." No results-section expansion needed if clearly flagged as derivation from reported β.
   - Ripple risk: Results (minor — add one derived sentence if option a chosen)

2. **[DISC-02]** severity: **critical**
   - Problem: Unpublished personal observation is introduced as supporting evidence in the Discussion: "Large *Pocillopora* colonies suffered disproportionate mortality during the 2024 Mo'orea heatwave (A. Stier, personal observation)." This is new data that did not appear in Results, Methods, or the abstract. At JAE, a personal observation used to support a causal claim is either cited properly (as a published source or explicitly flagged as anecdotal) or omitted. As currently written it functions to validate the biotic insurance hypothesis, which is a role it cannot legitimately play in an observational paper.
   - Location: Discussion section "Colony size, heatwaves, and the feedback loop," paragraph 2, sentence 3
   - Proposed fix: Either (a) remove the personal observation entirely and soften the sentence to "Size-dependent coral mortality during marine heatwaves is well-documented (Speare et al. 2022), and density dilution could represent an additional, previously unrecognised contributing factor"; or (b) if the 2024 Mo'orea data are to be used, they require a proper citation (e.g., preprint, dataset DOI) and a Methods entry. Do not use personal observation to support a mechanistic hypothesis.
   - Ripple risk: None if removed; Methods + Results if data are formally included

3. **[DISC-03]** severity: **major**
   - Problem: The limitations section is structurally absent — limitations are embedded as qualifications within substantive paragraphs rather than consolidated or consequentially stated. The most important rival explanation for the richness→condition association — that richness is a proxy for some unmeasured third variable (e.g., colony age, site-level larval supply, unmeasured microhabitat diversity) — is never named. The reader is told the causal attribution is uncertain but not what the alternative causal story would be.
   - Location: BEF section ("We cannot resolve this ambiguity…"), and opening paragraph caveat
   - Proposed fix: Add one sentence in the BEF paragraph naming the specific rival interpretation: "An alternative is that richness and condition both respond to a common driver — colony age, site-specific larval supply, or unmeasured microhabitat complexity — without any direct richness→condition effect. Our cross-sectional design cannot distinguish complementarity from unmeasured confounding." This makes the consequence concrete (the entire BEF interpretation hinges on excluding these alternatives) rather than just noting "observational design."
   - Ripple risk: None

4. **[DISC-04]** severity: **major**
   - Problem: The neighborhood null result (n_neighbors not significant for CAFI abundance or condition) receives no Discussion treatment. It appears only in the Results supplement section. Given that Q4 (neighborhood effects) was identified in the Methods as a prespecified analysis with a power note, readers need an interpretive statement: was the null result a true absence of neighborhood effects, or did the underpowered n=61 subsample prevent detection? The Methods explicitly noted 65% power for medium effects, making this limitation consequential.
   - Location: Missing from Discussion entirely; should follow or be incorporated into composition/BEF paragraphs
   - Proposed fix: Add 2–3 sentences in the "Composition, co-occurrence, and the BEF question" section or as a new brief paragraph: "The null result for neighborhood density (Q4) likely reflects insufficient power rather than a genuine absence of effects — 65% power at medium effect sizes leaves substantial uncertainty. However, the significant negative association between mean neighbor distance and richness (β = −0.005, p = 0.001) suggests spatial proximity to neighbors may matter more than raw count of neighbors, pointing to a source-sink interpretation that deserves replication at larger sample sizes."
   - Ripple risk: None

5. **[DISC-05]** severity: **major**
   - Problem: The opening paragraph buries the take-home and front-loads a caveat. "Colony size is the dominant axis structuring CAFI communities on *Pocillopora*" is a structural observation about what predicts what, not the paper's deepest insight. The Introduction's thesis was the feedback chain: habitat size → community assembly → habitat condition. The Discussion should open by asserting what was found about that chain, not by naming the predictor variable. The caveat in sentence 4 ("This loop is not empirically closed") arrives before the reader has internalized the finding, undermining forward momentum.
   - Location: Opening paragraph, sentence 1 and sentence 4
   - Proposed fix: Restructure the opening to lead with the chain result: "CAFI communities on *Pocillopora* operate as a partial feedback loop: colony size shapes community diversity through sublinear occupant scaling, and that diversity covaries — modestly but consistently — with coral physiological condition. Abundance scales at half-power with colony volume, density dilutes as corals grow, and composition turns over rather than nests along the size gradient. Whether these associations constitute a true causal feedback or reflect unmeasured confounding cannot be resolved from our cross-sectional data, but the pattern is consistent across three independent tests and survives rarefaction." Then continue to literature. Moving the caveat from sentence 4 to sentence 3 and reframing it around the chain rather than the individual predictor strengthens the opening without eliminating epistemic honesty.
   - Ripple risk: None

6. **[DISC-06]** severity: **minor**
   - Problem: The *Galeropsis monodonta* crawling-bypass mechanism is asserted without a citation and carries the full explanatory weight for why gastropods are the lone exception to sublinear scaling. "consistent with the hypothesis that *Galeropsis monodonta* colonises primarily by crawling between adjacent corals rather than through pelagic larval delivery — which would exempt it from signal-mediated redirection." This is plausible but unmarked speculation presented as an established mechanism.
   - Location: Scaling section, paragraph 3 ("The taxon-specificity of scaling exponents…"), sentence 3
   - Proposed fix: Add explicit speculation signal: "…which would exempt it from signal-mediated redirection — though whether *Galeropsis* recruitment is primarily crawl-mediated or larval remains untested." Alternatively, if there is a citation for Coralliophiline colonization mode, add it; if not, flag as speculative per the skill rule.
   - Ripple risk: None

7. **[DISC-07]** severity: **minor**
   - Problem: Proportionality imbalance: the scaling section (paragraphs 2–5 of section "Sublinear scaling and species stacking") is substantially longer than the BEF section (Q3), which carries the paper's most consequential and novel finding — a BEF signal in a habitat-occupant system. The imbalance communicates that mechanistic elaboration of a well-established pattern (sublinear scaling) matters more than the new, tentative, consequential finding (diversity–condition feedback), which inverts what the Introduction set up as the paper's contribution.
   - Location: Sections "Sublinear scaling and species stacking" (paragraphs 2–5) vs. "Composition, co-occurrence, and the BEF question" (paragraphs 3–5)
   - Proposed fix: Consider condensing the Abele & Patton isometric scaling conversion argument to 1–2 sentences (it is methodologically interesting but tangential to the paper's main argument) and expanding the BEF section to include: (a) specific prior BEF studies in habitat-occupant systems for comparison, and (b) the concrete alternative interpretations named in DISC-03. This rebalances the Discussion toward the paper's stated novel contribution.
   - Ripple risk: None; purely a restructuring issue

8. **[DISC-08]** severity: **minor**
   - Problem: The heatwave paragraph introduces "well-documented" for size-dependent mortality without a second citation beyond Speare et al. (2022). For a JAE audience (broad ecological, not coral-specialist), this should include at least one additional reference establishing the pattern, especially since the paragraph is building toward the novel biotic-insurance hypothesis that depends on this prior work being accepted.
   - Location: "Colony size, heatwaves, and the feedback loop," paragraph 2, sentence 1: "Size-dependent coral mortality during marine heatwaves is well-documented (Speare et al. 2022)"
   - Proposed fix: Add 1–2 supporting citations: e.g., Hughes et al. (2018) or a Mo'orea-specific reference if available. "[cite MCR-LTER / Edmunds / emerging work]" placeholder already exists in Discussion — this is the intended location. Resolve the placeholder.
   - Ripple risk: References section (add BibTeX entries)
